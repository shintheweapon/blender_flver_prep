# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Documentation
- `docs/NORMAL_MAPS.md` has figures, generated by `docs/figures/make_figures.py`: the layout of a normal map, the
  tangent frame in perspective, the directions in which the game reads green and red with tangents along +U, and an
  animated figure of what that does to a raised dome, and a figure of the ways out of Blender. The page explains
  `N × T` and the left-hand rule.
- The page and the README name FbxImporter's mirror setting per release: Transform in 1.11.0, a Mirror check box in
  1.7.0 to 1.10.2 (it mirrored X in 1.3 to 1.6.1).
- ER Tangents starts ticked for Elden Ring FLVERs only; the page says so and links the upstream pull request.
- A paper, `docs/paper/ER_NORMAL_MAPS.md`, sets out the convention, how it was measured, the maths with every formula
  explained in words, handedness, the common tangent convention and the game's, ER Tangents and matching the map
  instead, with references; the guide links to it, its section "What FbxImporter writes" is now "Tangents in the
  common convention", and Figs. 3 and 4 title those panels "Tangents along +U". The paper describes the importer and
  Blender's own operations in general terms, with its own versions of Figs. 1 and 3 to 5; the guide keeps the tool
  settings. The paper uses standard mathematical and graphics terms (vector, scalar, component, normalised,
  tangent-space normal). It states the modding community's view that blue holds gloss, untested here, shows how the
  vanilla frame was measured, with the check as a tested function (`docs/figures/frame_checks.py`), and includes
  screenshots of the two in-game tests. It describes ER Tangents as an import step, with or without it, rather than as
  a check box.
- The paper states how handedness shows on screen (with x to the right and y up, z out of the screen is
  right-handed) and has a figure of the axis swap from Blender into FLVER space, drawn by
  `docs/figures/paper_figures.py` from `docs/figures/axes.py`.
- The paper's worked example has a figure: the triangle in UV space and in game, with its partial derivatives and
  tangent frame.
- A figure in the paper shows the channel changes of Section VII at work on a raised dome: sideways or reflected from
  the map as authored, outward after the change; derived, not tested in game.
- The paper's layout test ran on the 1,437 part normal maps of the game (`docs/figures/integrability.py`, results in
  `docs/figures/integrability_maps.json`): Section IV-A gives a table per file-name prefix and a figure, and states the
  correlation as moderate rather than strong. The guide's summary of the test says the same.
- Every test in the paper (III-C, IV-A to IV-D, the validation of ER Tangents) opens with its question, why it
  matters and how the test answers it, and ends with its result and its impact on the implementation. IV-A says what
  it cannot tell: a map with both channels inverted, and how the game reads the maps.
- The paper's check of the vanilla tangent frame ran on the 1,515 part models of the game (`docs/figures/frame_survey.py`,
  results in `docs/figures/frame_survey.json`): Section IV-B gives a table per file-name prefix, and reports that
  meshes with a cloth material keep `W` at -1 on mirrored islands, unlike every other mesh and unlike ER Tangents;
  the cause and the effect in game were not tested. The guide's summary of the frame says the same.
- IV-B also compares that behaviour with the material file each mesh uses, which is what an importer sees: meshes
  whose material file contains "cloth" keep `W` at -1 on 96.0% of meshes and 98.1% of corners, the exceptions are
  described, and the paper mentions an optional import step that could copy the cloth frame.
- The paper follows the IEEE Editorial Style Manual on equations and tables: "Equation (n)" at the start of a sentence
  and "(n)" elsewhere, and every table numbered TABLE I, II and so on, with a caption above it and a citation in the
  text in order.
- The paper names none of this repository's files: its scripts and results are cited as a reference, and
  `docs/paper/README.md` explains how to reproduce Sections IV-A and IV-B, with the FLVER dumper
  (`docs/figures/flverdump/`, built against SoulsFormatsNEXT) and the survey driver (`docs/figures/survey_parts.py`)
  now in the repository. The references are numbered in order of first citation, and the paper names
  SoulsFormatsNEXT, the library it was read with.
- The paper states its scope as counts (1,437 normal maps, 1,515 part models, as installed), cites every figure in
  order with Fig. 3 placed after its first citation, defines ER Tangents before using it, refers to equations by
  number only, gives the direction of the 90° rotation (anticlockwise on the map), and writes figure captions in the
  IEEE form, without italics or a leading article.
- Fig. 3 of the paper has two parts. (a) is animated: a character's coordinates go through the rotation about X and
  the negation of Z in fixed axes drawn as Blender's Front view shows them, in the slanted projection of Fig. 2, with
  signed axis labels, up, forward and left labels that move with the character, a colour legend and a short text for
  each step. (b) is the three static stages. Both are drawn by `docs/figures/axis_swap_figure.py`. The paper and
  its figures call the facing direction "forward", as Blender's FBX settings do.
- The paper's references write dates day first, in UK order (for example "Accessed: 11 Nov. 2026").
- The guide mentions the fork's Cloth W check box for cloth materials.
- After a reading by readers new to it, the paper defines the importer's mirror setting and the sign of `d` (with the
  game's winding, `d` > 0 on mirrored islands; the worked example winds the other way), and reports both halves of
  the test plane in IV-D: in each import they are read alike, so the rotation, the reflection and the channel changes
  hold on mirrored islands too. Section V derives why the mirror turns red from -V to +V. The in-game claims match
  what was tested: one plane with ER Tangents, mirror setting not recorded. `tests/test_paper_readers.py` checks the
  new statements against the survey and the formulas.
- The paper also reads more easily: IV-A names its method (central differences, Pearson correlation, which pixels and
  tiles count) and splits the 40 maps that disagree (32 green inverted, 8 swapped); Table I and Fig. 4 say "Testable
  maps"; III-C says the anatomy fixes the handedness and the winding agrees; the in-game layout test is said to fix
  the sign of green, with IV-A giving the pairing; LOD0, matxml, UV island, the fallback's `W`, `û`, `v̂` and the
  importer's frame are defined before use; the Limitations add the unrecorded game version and the one test plane.
- The description of the dome figure (the guide's, and Fig. 7 in the paper) now says what the reflection about U = V
  does: normals on that diagonal stay outward, those on the other diagonal turn inward, and those along U and V turn
  sideways; the paper's sentence citing Fig. 7 says the same. The paper also states the identity behind the sign of
  `d`, explains why `W` per half swaps with the Z mirror, gives the reason the stored UVs have V = 0 at the top,
  limits Section V's measured claim to the FBX SDK's tangents and Section VII's mirrored islands to that route, says
  the IV-A tiles only decide whether a map is testable (threshold on values from 0 to 1), names Fig. 4's three
  outcomes, and says the in-game plane "looks alike" on both halves. It also names each IV-D half by its state after
  the import, writes out why `d` < 0 on unmirrored islands with the game's winding, and names the dome's diagonals
  (raised on U = V, dented on U = -V).
- The guide's claim that ER Tangents works for every Transform setting and every route now shows its basis: it says
  the frame follows the mesh after the Transform, names the settings the fork's unit tests cover (None, Mirror Z,
  Blender/3ds Max, Maya; not Mirror X, Mirror Y or Custom) and the imports that were checked (None and Mirror Z, a
  file without tangents), and its in-game check says the ticked plane "looks alike" on both halves.
- A video of the paper is being prepared in `docs/video/`: the English narration (`docs/video/script.en.md`, 16
  scenes of numbered segments, about 17 minutes, ending on the paper's references) and `docs/video/script.py`, which
  reads it and exports one text file per segment for a speech tool. Audio and renders are git-ignored.
- The video's renderer, `docs/video/make_video.py`: it times the narration from the audio files (or from the word
  count before they exist), captures the paper's figures, equations, tables, screenshots and references with
  headless Chrome, and joins them with ffmpeg into an MP4 with burned-in subtitles and an SRT file. The video ends on
  the paper's references, held for 10 seconds without narration. The video is dark, with Chrome set to dark mode
  so the figures always use their dark colours; subtitles are 20% larger on an opaque box; scene headings are larger;
  and `--until SECONDS` renders only the start of the video. Where the narration speaks of "the three panels below",
  the video shows Fig. 3(b)'s panels under the animation, as in the paper. The narration gives the paper's reasons
  for testing handedness on one model.
- `docs/paper/export_docx.py` exports the paper to a Word file for Google Docs: all equations as native Word
  equations, table captions above their tables, still figures as PNGs in light mode and Figs. 3(a) and 7 as
  animated GIFs (`docs/paper/README.md` says how to open it as a Google Doc). The video's Chrome helper takes a
  `color_scheme` for this. The tables carry their own grid lines, which pandoc's Word output leaves out, on every
  cell too, so the line under the header row shows in Google Docs; they are centred with centred captions, have a
  bold header row, and each stays on one page.
- The paper in Simplified Chinese, `docs/paper/ER_NORMAL_MAPS.zh-CN.md`, with its figures in Chinese
  (`docs/figures/*.zh.svg`, from the catalogue `docs/figures/zh_text.py`) and a glossary of the Chinese terms
  (`docs/paper/glossary.zh-CN.md`). The two papers link to each other, and tests check that the Chinese one carries
  the English one's equations, tables, figures, citations, references and counts. The Chinese paper names the games
  in Chinese (艾尔登法环 and the others), with the English name in brackets at first use, and its wording was revised to read as natural Chinese. The Chinese paper and figures name the channels as
  channels (红色通道, 绿色通道 and so on), since a bare 红色 reads as the colour. Reflections are named 镜像变换, or 关于直线 U = V 对称,
  rather than 反射, which reads as the reflection of light. The Chinese paper and figures use the terms of Chinese
  graphics writing: DirectX 格式, 翻转绿色通道 and 细节层次.
- Both papers say why the handedness of FLVER space was not checked on more models: it has long been held in the
  FromSoftware modding community that the games' model space is left-handed, and a game is unlikely to use two
  handednesses at once. Neither reason was tested; the Limitations point to Section III-C.
- Fig. 10's lower labels no longer run into the domes' arrows, in English and Chinese.
- The video in Simplified Chinese: `docs/video/script.zh-CN.md`, built with `make_video.py zh-CN` from the Chinese
  paper and figures, in Source Han Sans SC (or Noto Sans SC). `docs/video/languages.py` holds what differs per
  language. Chinese subtitles break between words (jieba, with the glossary's terms), preferably after punctuation.
  In both videos each change of subtitle moves to the nearest pause in the speech, and a partial render keeps the
  subtitles of its last segment in step.
- English subtitles change at sentence ends, where the narration pauses, instead of after every two lines; a
  sentence longer than two lines is cut into even parts, at a nearby comma where there is one, and the two lines
  of each subtitle are about equal in length (`subtitles.sentence_groups`, `subtitles.balanced`, switched on per
  language in `languages.py`).
- The Chinese paper exports to Word too (`export_docx.py --lang zh-CN`, `ER_NORMAL_MAPS.zh-CN.docx`), with its tables
  captioned 表 I and so on, its Chinese figures, and Chinese text in Noto Sans SC. Both Word files now number every
  page at the bottom and leave out the line linking the other language. The edits made after pandoc are in
  `docs/paper/docx_xml.py`.
- In both Word files, numbers in maths and the equation numbers are upright, and table captions are upright in
  small capitals, as IEEE sets them. The Chinese paper sets 文字表述： and its AI line in plain text, since italic
  Chinese is a slant of upright type.
- The Word tables have columns sized to their content, so no word breaks inside a cell, with 0.05 in cell margins;
  the pages are US Letter with 0.75 in margins; figures and tables are centred, no figure is taller than 7 in, and
  each stays on the page of its caption. In the Chinese file, the bracketed part of 表 I and 表 II's first column
  has its own line. The Word edits are split by subject into `docx_tables.py`, `docx_styles.py` and `docx_xml.py`.
- `export_docx.py --lang zh-CN --math-images` writes `ER_NORMAL_MAPS.zh-CN.math-images.docx`, with every maths
  expression as a picture drawn by KaTeX, for viewers that do not show Word equations: inline maths lowered onto the
  baseline, display equations centred with their number at the right margin. The steps are in
  `docs/paper/math_images.py`.

## 1.0.0

### Added
- **Prepare and Export...** and **Export Copy...** buttons in the FLVER Prep tab: write the `<armature>_fs` collection
  to an FBX file as it is (same vertex positions, normals and corner order; identity object transforms), using Blender's
  FBX exporter with Forward Y, Up Z, no Apply Transform and FBX Units Scale. Names keep the `_fs` suffix.
- The report after an export names the Mirror Z setting to use in FbxImporter; Prepare Copy now records its options
  on the collection for this.
- Prepare Copy shows the armature copy In Front, so its bones are drawn over the meshes.
- The extension declares the `files` permission.
- **Y-Up Views** in the FLVER Prep tab: six buttons turn the 3D Viewport to a fixed orthographic view that shows the
  Y-up copies upright, from -Z, +Z, -X, +X, +Y and -Y. The Z views are named **Face** and **Back** after the side of the
  character they show, from the Mirror Z the active object's `_fs` collection was made with.
- **Side Up** (Y-Up or Z-Down) for the Left and Right views, remembered as an add-on preference.
- **Anti-clockwise** and **Clockwise** buttons: turn the current view 90°, as Blender's Shift+Numpad 4 and 6 do.
- **Remove Vertex Colors** button in the FLVER Prep tab: removes every Color Attribute from the selected meshes or the
  active mesh, optionally keeping the render color. Both options are add-on preferences.
- `docs/NORMAL_MAPS.md`: Elden Ring's normal-map layout and tangent convention, and how to get matching tangents
  through FbxImporter.
- The manifest links to the repository (`website`).

### Changed
- The reports and README name FbxImporter 1.11's **Transform** setting (None or Mirror Z) in place of its
  old Mirror check box.
- Named **FLVER Prep** (was FS FBX Tools); extension id `flver_prep`, sidebar tab **FLVER Prep**. Install it in
  place of FS FBX Tools, whose add-on preferences do not carry over.

### Fixed
- Command tooltips show in Chinese (Blender looks them up in the operator context).
- Prepare Copy keeps the originals' collections: each is recreated inside the `_fs` collection as `<collection>_fs`,
  below the collections the armature and all its meshes share. Before, every copy went straight into the `_fs`
  collection. Export includes the copies in the recreated collections, and running Prepare Copy again removes them.

## 0.1.0

### Added
- **Prepare Copy**, in the 3D Viewport sidebar tab "FS FBX": copies the active armature and its meshes into the
  collection `<armature>_fs`, converted into the space The12thAvenger's FbxImporter reads (Y up, metres, identity
  object transforms). Meshes are the armature's children and the meshes whose Armature modifier targets it.
- Meshes are copied in the armature's rest pose with their other modifiers applied; materials are shared; vertex
  groups and bone names are kept; copied objects end in `_fs`.
- **Mirror Z** and **Flip Faces** options, matching FbxImporter's options of the same names, remembered as add-on
  preferences (off by default).
- Running it again on the same armature replaces its previous `_fs` collection.
- Simplified Chinese interface (zh_HANS), using Blender's own words.
